# AI Is Creating Unprecedented Opportunity. Who Will It Belong To?

## What is happening?

AI is giving individuals capabilities that once required a large company or a well-funded team. One person can learn a subject, write software, test an idea, analyse options, or turn a rough thought into a working plan much faster than before.

These tools remain unreliable and cannot remove every physical or social limit. Yet useful knowledge and skilled assistance are becoming cheaper, allowing one person to attempt far more than a few years ago.

## What is the problem?

Access to AI is not the same as control over it. The most capable models, computing infrastructure, data, and distribution channels belong to a small number of companies. Their owners set the priorities, limits, data policies, and terms of access.

This does not make every AI company an enemy. It does concentrate power. If AI produces far more wealth while most people remain customers, employees, or data sources, infrastructure owners gain extraordinary leverage. Proprietary AI could make everyone more productive while leaving most people dependent on rules they cannot inspect or change.

## What do we propose?

Open Abundance is an attempt to build an open coordination layer where AI expands the possibilities of every participant, not only the wealth and power of a platform owner.

AI should help people understand what they want, find knowledge and collaborators, coordinate resources, and turn intention into results. The measure of success is whether people can better meet their needs and pursue the lives they choose.

## Three views of the AI future

Several recent essays describe a shift much larger than better chatbots. They are forecasts, not established facts, and their authors have not endorsed Open Abundance.

In *Machines of Loving Grace*, Dario Amodei describes how powerful AI could accelerate health, science, economic development, governance, and work. He also notes that progress still depends on physical resources, institutions, reliable data, and human action.

In *The Gentle Singularity*, Sam Altman argues that intelligence and energy may become abundant in the 2030s. He also calls for access to superintelligence to be widely distributed rather than concentrated in one person, company, or country.

In *Personal Superintelligence for Everyone*, Mark Zuckerberg presents AI as a tool for personal agency. Each person could have an AI that understands their goals and helps them create, learn, connect, and act.

Together, these essays point to three forms of abundance: greater capability, widely available intelligence, and more power in the hands of individuals. Open Abundance starts with the next question: how do those individuals coordinate with one another?

## Intelligence does not automatically produce a result

Suppose someone wants to change careers, start a small business, find a better place to live, or learn a practical skill. An AI assistant can explain the options and produce a plan. A personal AI can go further by understanding the person's experience, time, money, and constraints.

But many goals still depend on other people. Someone may need a first customer, a partner, a mentor, a team, a shared resource, or a group with the same demand. They need trust, agreement, and follow-through—not just advice.

Open Abundance is meant to connect these pieces. A personal AI helps an individual understand and act on a goal. A shared coordination layer finds compatible needs, skills, knowledge, and resources across many participants. The final decision remains with the people involved.

## Why ownership matters

People who hold power do not stop having interests of their own. Leaders answer to investors, institutions protect themselves, and platforms favour the goals they were built to serve. Control tends to reinforce itself.

AI can magnify this pattern. A system that understands millions of people, directs attention, and allocates opportunities gives its owner enormous leverage.

An AI Coordinator is not automatically neutral. Governed behind closed doors, it may become a more efficient instrument of the same hierarchy. To serve people equally, its purpose and rules must be public, access open, decisions explainable, and participants free to challenge them or leave.

## What Open Abundance means in practice

Open Abundance begins from a simple principle: in relation to the AI Coordinator, all people belong to one class.

There should be no owner class whose goals matter more and no user class living under rules written for someone else's benefit. Wealth, status, or early access should not grant control over another person's path. Everyone follows the same public rules, chooses their own goals, and may refuse a recommendation.

Classless does not mean identical people, identical outcomes, or the end of personal property and independent projects. People have different skills, needs, and ambitions. Those differences should not become permanent power over others.

The Coordinator's objective should not be profit, engagement, or obedience. Its purpose is to help people meet their needs and pursue their wishes without reducing other people's possibilities. It should connect skills with needs, combine shared demand, and make successful paths easier for others to follow.

Open access is essential. Participants should understand the rules, bring their own goals and projects, use compatible tools, and benefit from shared knowledge without surrendering control of their lives.

The long-term direction is a post-scarcity society. Resources will not become infinite, but better production and coordination can prevent more human needs from going unmet because access and opportunity are badly organised.

The economic model, governance, safeguards, and technology still have to be tested in the open. The practical test is whether people gain more real options, more control over their lives, and a better chance to achieve what matters to them.
